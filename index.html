        About this monitor

        Inspired by Eric Dollard's view of the Earth as an electrical system — telluric earth currents in the crust, the ionosphere as the upper plate of a planetary capacitor — this app watches those signals over Florida and flags when they deviate from their own recent baseline.

        How the index works. Each local signal (telluric |E|, TEC anomaly, ULF Z/H ratio) is scored as a robust z-score against its trailing 7 days. The index is the RMS of those scores. If Kp ≥ 5 or Dst ≤ −50 nT, the sun is driving the disturbance and the alert is downgraded to "storm".

        Forecasts. Every alert is written down as a testable prediction (magnitude, radius, time window) and scored automatically against USGS. The scorecard compares the hit rate with the chance of a quake turning up by luck alone, which is about 14% per 7-day window for M2.5+ within 800 km of central Florida. A method that really predicts quakes has to beat that number over many alerts.

        Honest caveat. No precursor method — telluric, ionospheric or otherwise — has yet shown reliable, repeatable earthquake prediction, and Florida is one of the least seismically active places in the U.S. Treat this as an experiment: the logged history and the USGS quake overlay let you judge for yourself whether anomalies ever line up with real events. Never use it for safety decisions.

        Data: NOAA SWPC (geoelectric field model, GloTEC, Kp, Dst, DSCOVR/ACE solar wind), USGS Geomagnetism Program, USGS Earthquake Hazards Program. The telluric values are NOAA's modeled geoelectric field (driven by real magnetometer data), not direct electrode measurements.
